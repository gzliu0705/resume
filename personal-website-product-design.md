# Personal Website Product Design Spec

## 1. Project Goal

Create a personal website that functions as a **creative, memorable, recruiter-friendly narrative resume**.

This is **not** a standard personal blog and **not** a generic portfolio. The website should help three audiences understand Guanzhang Liu quickly and deeply:

1. **Recruiters** — should be able to understand the candidate in under 30 seconds.
2. **Hiring Managers** — should be able to understand problem-solving ability, domain depth, and project impact within a few minutes.
3. **Peers / Potential Teammates** — should be able to sense working style, thinking patterns, and personality.

The website should establish a strong personal identity while remaining clear, international, and professionally credible.

---

## 2. Core Positioning

### Core brand direction
**The Interpreter of Systems**

Guanzhang is not framed simply as “someone who does machine learning,” but as someone who:
- works inside complex real-world systems,
- finds useful signal from noise,
- translates technical complexity into actionable decisions,
- brings scientific rigor into applied industrial and business settings.

### Supporting expression
A possible operating sentence for the site:

> Turning complex systems into signals people can act on.

This expression should guide the structure and tone of the website.

---

## 3. Narrative Device

### Main symbolic object
**A cat**

The cat is not merely decorative. It serves as the main narrative device that threads the site together.

### Why the cat works
The cat symbolizes:
- observation,
- curiosity,
- quiet intelligence,
- sensitivity to weak signals,
- movement through layers and traces,
- playful but focused attention.

This aligns naturally with the site’s deeper concept: interpreting systems.

### Role of the cat on the website
The cat should act primarily as:
- **observer**,
- **guide**,
- light narrative companion.

It should **not** feel childish, mascot-like, or overly cute in a commercial/cartoon way. The target balance is:
- **70% professional**
- **30% playful**

So the cat should feel intelligent, minimal, and gently charming.

---

## 4. Product Principles

The website should follow these principles:

### 4.1 Clear before clever
No matter how creative the storytelling is, the website must communicate the basics immediately:
- who Guanzhang is,
- what he does,
- what kind of roles he is open to,
- why he is differentiated,
- how to contact him.

### 4.2 Creativity through concept, not clutter
The site should feel creative because of:
- strong concept,
- intentional naming,
- visual storytelling,
- interaction flow,
- editorial rhythm.

It should **not** rely on:
- excessive animation,
- heavy illustration overload,
- unclear navigation,
- too many decorative gimmicks.

### 4.3 Layered reading experience
The site should support two reading speeds:
- **Layer 1: quick scan** for recruiters
- **Layer 2: immersive exploration** for hiring managers and peers

### 4.4 International, not too local
The site should avoid feeling too China-local in wording, design language, or framing. It should read naturally to an international hiring audience, especially London-based recruiters and managers.

### 4.5 Thoughtful, playful, unconventional
The overall personality should feel:
- intellectually playful,
- thoughtful,
- unconventional,
- calm,
- clean,
- self-aware.

It should avoid:
- academic stiffness,
- startup hype,
- AI-bro energy,
- excessive formality,
- excessive cuteness.

---

## 5. Audience Strategy

### 5.1 Recruiters
Primary needs:
- understand role fit quickly,
- see current status and target market,
- identify relevant experience,
- access resume and LinkedIn,
- know location / openness to London / work authorization context.

Design response:
- immediate summary on first screen,
- strong hierarchy,
- quick facts section,
- clear CTA buttons,
- concise supporting language.

### 5.2 Hiring Managers
Primary needs:
- understand scope and difficulty of work,
- assess problem-solving quality,
- evaluate technical + business judgment,
- understand how Guanzhang thinks.

Design response:
- selected work section,
- project summaries in a structured half-expanded format,
- “How I Think” section,
- point-of-view section,
- timeline with context.

### 5.3 Peers / Potential Teammates
Primary needs:
- understand style of collaboration,
- sense values and personality,
- see whether working together would be interesting.

Design response:
- more human and reflective copy,
- beliefs / notes section,
- cat-based narrative warmth,
- thoughtful writing tone.

## 6. Site Type

The website should be designed as a hybrid of:
- **narrative resume**,
- **editorial portfolio**,
- **future project archive**.

It should **not** start as a blog-first website.

A blog or notes system can be added later, but the primary product is a **curated personal website with story structure**.

## 8. Page Model

The site should begin as a **single long-form scrolling homepage** with strong section identity.

### Why this is the right model
- supports storytelling,
- supports recruiter scanning,
- keeps the experience simple,
- allows strong visual flow,
- lets the cat motif guide movement and continuity.

### Future page system
Later, the site can expand into:
- `/work/thermal-risk-ranking`
- `/work/battery-degradation`
- `/work/work-order-nlp`
- `/work/ip-popularity-scoring`
- `/notes/...`

But phase 1 should prioritize the homepage narrative.

## 9. Scroll Narrative Strategy

The website should feel like a guided descent through layers of identity and work.

### Reading logic
The user enters through clarity, then gradually moves into depth.

Flow should feel like:
1. **I know who this person is.**
2. **I know what this person does.**
3. **I see the path they took.**
4. **I see the work they have done.**
5. **I understand how they think.**
6. **I know how to reach out.**

### Cat-based motion concept
As the user scrolls, the cat can:
- move along a path line,
- appear in corners observing a system map,
- sit beside important insights,
- track along the timeline,
- subtly transition from section to section.

The motion should be light and purposeful, not distracting.

## 10.3 Guided Entry

### Purpose
Give users a sense of control and orientation within a long scrolling page.

### Recommended wording
Start anywhere:
- Follow my path
- See selected work
- Read how I think

### Function
These can scroll-jump to relevant sections.

### Why this matters
In a long narrative site, users should not feel trapped in a linear scroll. This guided entry helps bridge storytelling with usability.

### Design notes
- This is one of the site’s more editorial moments.
- Good place for subtle cat interaction.
- Could feel like a small map or branching trail.

---

## 10.4 Path & Traces

### Purpose
Combine timeline and selected work into one synchronized narrative block.

### Core idea
The left side shows the trajectory.
The right side shows the corresponding context, work, or signal.

### Left column: path
Possible milestones:
- Nanjing University — Materials Science
- Computational Materials Science — research / scientific computing
- ByteDance — market strategy and IP analytics
- Li Auto — battery intelligence and applied ML
- Open to London — next chapter

### Right column: traces / key signals
Each stage reveals one key artifact or work fragment:
- Skeaf / scientific tooling / research thinking
- IP popularity scoring system
- thermal runaway risk ranking
- quasi-causal battery degradation system
- work-order NLP classification

### Interaction concept
As the user scrolls:
- the active timeline item highlights,
- the right-side panel changes,
- the cat may move along the timeline path,
- small supporting copy adds context.

### Why this is valuable
This makes the site feel less like a flat resume and more like a story of evolving problem spaces.

## 10.6 How I Think

### Purpose
Translate work history into a recognizable working mind.

### Suggested content themes
- trust and interpretability matter,
- messy systems are often where the real problem lives,
- the value of a model depends on whether people can act on it,
- technical ideas improve through pressure-tested conversation,
- domain understanding is part of modeling.

### Format recommendation
4–6 short thought blocks rather than a long essay.

### Example direction
- I’m drawn to systems where the signal is weak but the decision still matters.
- I care about trustworthy models, not just impressive metrics.
- I like translating technical detail into decisions people can actually use.
- I work best where ideas can be challenged openly and improved together.

### Design notes
- Could feel like annotations, field notes, or margin notes.
- Strong place to deepen the intellectual identity of the site.

## 11. Skills Strategy

### Should skills appear on the homepage?
**Yes, but not as a traditional skills section.**

### What should be avoided
Avoid:
- long skill lists near the top,
- dense tag clouds,
- resume-like categories in the first screen.

### Recommended approach
Skills should appear in three forms:

#### 1. Identity chips in hero
Examples:
- Applied ML
- Risk Modeling
- Feature Systems
- NLP
- Causal Inference

#### 2. Embedded in project summaries
Each project naturally reveals methods and tools.

#### 3. Optional later section
If needed, include a lighter section named:
- Toolkit
- Ways I Build
- Methods I Work With

This should be lower on the page or on a separate resume page.

## 14. Interaction Design Direction

### Interaction style
Subtle, smooth, story-supportive.

### Recommended interaction moments
- smooth scroll transitions,
- timeline highlight on scroll,
- section reveal through motion,
- guided entry click-to-section transitions,
- light hover responses on cards,
- gentle cat movement or pose change between sections.

### What to avoid
- long loading intro,
- overly game-like interactions,
- scroll hijacking,
- too many parallax effects,
- distracting animated mascots.

### Ideal interaction principle
Motion should reward attention, not demand it.

## 17. Success Criteria

The website is successful if:

### For recruiters
Within 30 seconds, they can answer:
- Who is this person?
- What does he do?
- Is he relevant for the role?
- Is he open to London opportunities?
- How do I contact him?

### For hiring managers
Within 3–5 minutes, they can answer:
- Does he solve meaningful, difficult problems?
- Can he work across technical and business layers?
- Does he show judgment, clarity, and depth?
- Is there a strong reason to interview him?

### For peers
They should feel:
- this person is thoughtful,
- this person has taste and point of view,
- this person seems interesting to work with.
