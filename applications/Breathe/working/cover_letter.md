Dear Breathe team,

I am writing to express my interest in joining Breathe's talent community. My route into battery data science began in computational materials research at Nanjing University, where I developed a 3D tool connecting calculated quantum-oscillation orbits to the regions of a Fermi surface they came from. That work taught me to value not only a sound calculation, but also making its meaning clear enough for others to examine and use.

I have carried that approach into my work as a Data Scientist in Li Auto's Battery Intelligence Department. Working with BMS, cell-design, performance and after-sales colleagues, I analyse real-world battery degradation, forecast state of health and evaluate targeted over-the-air strategies. Fleet data rarely offer simple answers: apparent drivers may be confounded, and a strategy intended to extend battery life must also account for its effects on drivers. My role has been to bring careful analysis into engineering decisions without claiming more certainty than the evidence supports. In battery safety, I developed an explainable risk-ranking system and monthly scoring pipeline to help teams prioritise investigations of rare thermal events, while keeping the distinction between a ranking and a failure prediction clear.

Breathe's work on physics-based battery management software speaks to the next question I want to work on: how sound battery science becomes something useful in real products. My experience is in the behaviour of batteries across a fleet and in building data systems that engineering teams can use, rather than in developing physics-based controls. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss whether that perspective would be useful to Breathe, and to learn from the people building the software itself.

Yours sincerely,  
Wentworth Liu
